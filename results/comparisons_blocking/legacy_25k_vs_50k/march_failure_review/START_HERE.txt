Question answered
Does minimum withdrawal reduce synchronized failure when each account holds one position, and
did replacements really restore the book quickly?

What we learned
The March 30, 2026 trade kills no PA in any of the 14 replays: blocked books enter it with
$696–$810 of profit equity instead of $101, so the non-blocking cliff came from stacked
overlapping losses. Synchronized failure remains at lower reserves — the $5,700 daily-maximum
books lose all 20 PAs together on March 26, and the daily-minimum winners lose theirs on March
5–6, ending with 10 (25K) and 16 (50K). The $6,700 maximum books keep earning after the event,
$18,933 and $19,047, where the non-blocking ones received nothing.

How to interpret it
Reserves and pipelines are frozen as in the non-blocking review; the two minimum winners come
from the blocked pipeline study and reproduce it, including per-trade account assignments. Four
daily-maximum books are unchanged by the evaluation pause, against two without blocking. The
minimum runs receive no closing withdrawal: their PAs had just been paid, so the firm’s
minimum-trading-days rule denies the request.
