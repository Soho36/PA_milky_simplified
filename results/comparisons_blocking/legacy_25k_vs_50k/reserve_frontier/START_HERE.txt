Question answered
With daily maximum fixed and each account holding one position, where is the reserve survival
boundary and how much cash does extra cushion defer?

What we learned
With the pipelines frozen as in the non-blocking study, the March 30, 2026 trade no longer
kills the book: no PA dies that day from $6,100 for either product, against $6,780.11 without
blocking, and at $6,700 all 20 PAs survive it. Blocked PAs reach that trade with $696–$810 of
profit equity instead of $101. Daily-maximum cash is about half the non-blocking level: at
$6,800 total net is $312,331 (25K) and $315,651 (50K), against $629,920 and $641,765. The best
ongoing reserve drops to $4,000 (25K) and $1,000 (50K). Across annual cold starts, aged-PA
deaths stop from $6,100–$6,200; in the 50K full history, four PAs at least a year old still die
even at $10,000.

How to interpret it
Pipelines, reserve grid, $10 and cent-level probes and windows are frozen from the non-blocking
study, where the pipelines came from its shared-seat daily-maximum search, so only the copying
rule differs. Six saved pipeline rows reproduce, including per-trade assignments, and no
replayed PA held two trades at once. The chart is not drawn (ReportLab is missing), and the
non-blocking folder’s one-off validation files have no counterpart.
