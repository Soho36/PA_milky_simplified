Partition, not dispersion

Across the whole dataset, RR mixing does reduce death clustering. Not by staggering deaths through time — each sleeve still dies as one block on one day — but by cutting the block into pieces. The dose is the width of the spread, and a narrow spread buys nothing.

Holds

Over 11 operating cells, the worst single day costs a mixture 67% of its live book against 82% for a single-RR book. wide is lowest of all thirteen at 47%, and beats RR 1.00 in 11 of 11 cells.

Dose

Spread width drives when sleeves are hit; sleeve count drives how many die together. near has neither — 0.5 of width, two sleeves — and beats RR 1.00 in only 1 of 11.

Open

Composition, width and sleeve count are still confounded, the eleven cells are one history rather than eleven, and no mixture beats the best single RR chosen with hindsight in more than 5 of 11.

Share of the live book lost in the worst single trading day, averaged over 11 operating cells — six fresh starts crossed with the daily-maximum and MFE-first sensitivities. The lighter tick is the same figure with the 2020 start removed, which takes COVID out of the window.

Mixtures average 0.668 against 0.815 for single-RR books, on slightly fewer deaths (32.6 against 34.3), so the improvement is not bought by dying more often. Dropping 2020 narrows the gap to 0.685 against 0.746 but does not close it — COVID amplifies the effect rather than creating it.

The dose: how wide, and how many sleeves

Each mixture against the single-RR average, on the two clustering measures that move independently.

Worst-day share of book — set by spread width

0.5 of width buys nothing. 1.0 and above works.

Largest same-signal loss — set by sleeve count

Two sleeves of ten still lose ten at once. Four sleeves halve it.

This is why modest and four each win one measure and lose the other: modest is wide but coarse, four is fine but narrow. wide is the only tested portfolio that is both, and it leads both columns.

Head to head, cell by cell

Averages can hide a portfolio that wins big once and loses often. Count of the 11 operating cells in which each mixture beats the named benchmark on worst-day share.

Beating the homogeneous median is the operationally meaningful test: you do not know in advance which single RR will turn out best. wide clears it every time. Beating the best homogeneous is hindsight, and nothing clears that reliably.

05

The mechanism, close up

One event, account by account: twenty funded seats on 1 March 2026 under the $5,700 daily-maximum stress policy. Illustrative of how the partition works — not the basis for the general claim above, which comes from all eleven cells.

Deaths per mixture equal sleeve size exactly, and land on a single day. wide loses its five RR 1.50 seats; four loses its RR 1.00 and RR 1.50 fives. Nothing is desynchronized within a sleeve — what the mixture buys is that the other sleeves are untouched.

06

How concentrated each book was before the event

Accounts sharing an identical headroom value on 1 March 2026. The largest such group is the number that can die on one signal — the count of distinct values is not, because a book can hold six singletons and one large clump.

Six of the nine single-RR books sat at one balance — twenty seats at the same headroom to the dollar. RR 0.75 holds seven distinct values, which looks dispersed until you see that 13 of its 20 share one. wide and four hold four groups of five: fewer distinct values, a far smaller worst case.

RR 2.50 beats wide on cash in all six starts. In the 2023 start it also beats it on deaths — 17 against 28, zero empty books either way. The mixture is not better than its best constituent; it is insurance against not knowing in advance which constituent that will be, and the head-to-head counts are the price of that insurance.

08

Isolation: what happens with no replacements

Same portfolios, replacement supply switched off, averaged over 11 cells. Everything eventually exhausts, so the question is not whether but how.

A single-RR book loses its entire remaining book in one same-signal event: 12.9 deaths on average, 12.93 of them simultaneous. Mixtures cap that at 6.6, and stay alive about 240 days longer. This is the clearest statement of the mechanism in the whole study.

10

Three corrections to the earlier reading

Distinct balances was the wrong metric

was: “RR 0.75 is more dispersed than any tested mixture”

Counting distinct values rewarded RR 0.75 for a tail of singletons while hiding a 13-account clump. By largest-group share — what actually sets how many die together — the mixtures are the better instrument at 25%, not the worse one. This reverses the earlier conclusion.

RR 0.50 is not generally weak

was: “the mixtures dilute a weak constituent”

It is the sole source of mixture deaths in the 2023 isolation run, but it takes zero damage in March 2026 and sits mid-field on whole-dataset clustering. Its weakness is period-specific. The composition confound stands; the characterisation of RR 0.50 does not.

near is not the only mixture with empty books

was: “the fourth, near, is the one that keeps its empty-book episodes”

modest has one and four has two across the six primary starts. wide alone has none. The heatmap was right; the sentence beside it was not.

11

What would sharpen this

Separate width from composition. wide is simultaneously the widest spread, a four-sleeve book, and the only one holding both RR 0.50 and RR 3.50. A 1.25 / 1.75 / 2.25 / 2.75 arm holds sleeve count and width roughly fixed while changing the constituents; a 0.50 / 3.50 two-sleeve arm holds width fixed while halving the count. Those two runs would decide which factor is doing the work.

Push the sleeve count. Largest same-signal loss tracks sleeve size almost exactly, so twenty sleeves of one seat is the limit case. That needs no new mechanism, only more distinct RR values in the book, and it is the cheapest way to find where the benefit flattens.

Run the reserve-ladder control. If the aim is that seats do not share a headroom value, a per-account reserve ladder disperses balances directly and costs nothing to test. Until it is run, the claim that RR is the right instrument for this is unproven — only that it is an instrument.
