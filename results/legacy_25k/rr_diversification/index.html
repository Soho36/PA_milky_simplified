Partition, not dispersion

The RR mixtures do something real, but it is not what the study set out to find. They shrink the size of a synchronized failure. They do not spread deaths across the calendar — every sleeve still dies as one block, on one day.

Holds

wide is the only portfolio of the thirteen with zero empty-book episodes in all six operating starts. Its own four constituents run up to six.

Reframe

Every March death in every mixture is an account on a doomed setting, and they all die on the same day. Blast radius falls; timing is untouched.

Confound

Five of nine single-RR books took zero March damage. The mixtures did worse than those five — and the one mixture without RR 0.50 is also the one that behaves like a single book.

01

The March 2026 event, account by account

Twenty funded seats per portfolio on 1 March 2026, grouped into RR sleeves. Red cells died during March. The $5,700 daily-maximum stress policy — not the primary $6,800 baseline, which has no March deaths at all.

Deaths per mixture equal the sleeve size exactly: wide loses its five RR 1.50 seats and nothing else; four loses its RR 1.00 and RR 1.50 fives. A pure RR 2.50 or RR 3.50 book walks through the event untouched. RR 0.75 is the only portfolio whose losses land on two different days.

02

How many distinct balances each book actually held

Distinct profit-equity values across the twenty live seats on 1 March 2026, four weeks before the event. Identical balances are what turns an $81.50 adverse excursion into twenty simultaneous deaths.

Six of the nine single-RR books sat at one distinct balance — all twenty seats at the same headroom to the dollar. Mixing lifts that to the number of sleeves, no further. But RR 0.75 alone reached seven distinct balances spanning $4,414, and RR 0.50 reached five spanning $2,633 — both more dispersed than any tested mixture, and both homogeneous.

03

Empty-book episodes across six operating starts

Times the book fell to zero live accounts after first activation. Daily minimum withdrawals, $6,800 headroom, $5,000 owner cash plus $200/month, shared 20-seat cap, MAE-first.

This is the finding that survives scrutiny. wide holds a clean zero row while RR 2.50 alone — the best single setting on cash — empties its book twelve times, six of them in the 2021 start. Continuity and cash pull in opposite directions here.

What the continuity costs

Net operating cash

RR 2.50 beats wide on cash in all six starts and on deaths in three. In the 2023 start it dominates outright — 17 deaths against 28, zero empty books either way, and $263,867 against $188,417. The mixture is not better than its best constituent; it is insurance against not knowing in advance which constituent that will be.

05

The control that is missing

Isolation phase, 2023 start, no replacements — the cleanest read in the study.

Eight of nine single-RR books lost nothing. Every death in every mixture was an RR 0.50 account. The improvement from twenty same-signal deaths down to five is the RR 0.50 allocation shrinking from twenty seats to five — arithmetic, not diversification. Three of the four tested mixtures contain RR 0.50; the fourth, near, is the one that keeps its empty-book episodes. Spread and “contains a weak setting” are confounded across the whole design.

07

What would actually test the idea

The goal was to spread deaths through time. Nothing here does that: within a sleeve, deaths are perfectly coincident, and which day a sleeve dies is chosen by the market, not staggered by design. Three things would separate the mechanism from the confound.

A mixture of only strong settings. Something like 1.25 / 1.50 / 2.00 / 2.50, with no RR 0.50 sleeve to carry the losses. If that still clears the empty-book episodes, the spread is doing the work. If it does not, the weak sleeve was.

More sleeves, thinner. Blast radius is sleeve size, so twenty sleeves of one seat is the limit case. That is the version of this idea worth pricing, and it needs no new mechanism — only more distinct RR values in the book.

A staggered-reserve arm as the cheap control. If the aim is simply that seats do not sit at one headroom, a per-account reserve ladder — $6,200, $6,400, $6,600 — disperses balances directly. RR 0.75 already reached seven distinct balances on its own, which suggests the dispersion channel is not really RR at all.
