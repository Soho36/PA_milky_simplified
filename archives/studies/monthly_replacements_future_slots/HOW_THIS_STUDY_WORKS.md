# How this study operates

Start with the selected cash budget. Attempt one new account monthly, capped
at 20 live accounts. Compare waiting for scheduled purchases against two ways
to replace deaths at the next midnight check:

- Advance replacements: buy the replacement when affordable and cancel one
  future monthly purchase for each successful replacement.
- Plus replacements: buy replacements in addition to scheduled monthly growth.

Deaths awaiting an affordable replacement remain pending. New deaths of replaced
accounts create new obligations. Multiple deaths can be replaced together if
cash and capacity permit. On a monthly boundary, old slot debt cancels the
current scheduled purchase first; replacements bought now consume later slots.
With no old debt, a replacement and a scheduled purchase can coincide.

The same four owner budgets and three withdrawal settings apply to all arms.
Monthly-one and weekly-one are reproduced controls. Each account trades while
alive; withdrawal checks begin in its second calendar month. The reserve is what
we leave after payouts, not a guaranteed minimum balance or a trading stop.

Net cash excludes contributions, subtracts account fees and includes the
applicable split. Total adds one permitted terminal request. Terminal receipts
are never used to buy more accounts. Slot debt outstanding at the horizon is
reported; it represents future skipped purchases, not an owner cash liability.

Average live accounts includes the ramp-up from the common opening month to
end of tape. FIFO death-to-purchase waits describe vacancies, not independent
causal replacement effects; inspect unmatched deaths as well as matched waits.
Faster replacements also change entry dates, future deaths and purchases.
