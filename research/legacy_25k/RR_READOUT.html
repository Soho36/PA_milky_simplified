Apex Legacy 25K PA · RR and GG studies through 2026-09-24 · decision and evidence

Partition, not dispersion

The decision has held up through every study: split the book across RR groups, keep RR 0.50 + RR 2.50 as the reference pair, and deploy RR 0.50 first. What the evidence supports is narrower than the early headlines. Grouping cuts the risk of a book emptying while it is being built; once a book is built, headroom is what protects it; and 0.50/2.50 is in the top group of pairs, not proven best.

What to do

  1. Split the book across RR groups rather than running a single RR setting. Grouped books emptied 2.2 times per portfolio across six starts; RR 1.00 books, uniform or with reserve ladders, 8.5.
  2. Use RR 0.50 + RR 2.50 as the reference pair. It ties for the fewest shared failed quarters among the 78 ordinary RR pairs. In a capitalised book both of its sleeves kept $2,400 or more of headroom through the worst event, while the RR 1.00 sleeve the triples add fell to $741.
  3. Deploy RR 0.50 first when starting a book. It had the fewest startup failures in every design tested. It reduces them; it does not always eliminate them.
  4. Stop tuning RR values. No pair of any family fails together less often than the product-of-marginals benchmark, and the best RR pairs are tied.
  5. Treat per-account headroom, not seat count, as the safety milestone. Seeded at $6,800 of frozen headroom, no allocation or single-setting book lost an account in 312 cases; at $1,500, even a full book of twenty could lose everything.

01

Grouping cuts establishment risk

Every empty-book episode in rr_followup's primary operating runs, split by the largest book size reached before it emptied. Daily minimum withdrawals, $6,800 mean reserve, $5,000 owner cash plus $200/month, 20-seat cap, six starts, MAE-first.

RR-grouped arms lose fewer books at both early stages: 12 against 24 at one to four PAs, 8 against 24 at five to nine, across 9 and 6 arms. Among these fifteen arms, the only books emptied after holding ten or more PAs are three uniform_low runs — a $5,300 reserve — on 2026-03-24, losing six to ten accounts at once. That is a reserve effect in the March event, not an RR effect.

02

What protects a full book: headroom, not seat count

mature_rr_book: 624 cases that start with twenty pre-owned PAs over a frozen $25,100 floor, at $6,800 or $1,500 of headroom each. Hold runs trade each of 26 quarters with no withdrawals or replacements; recovery runs follow up to twelve months under the daily-minimum policy with normal replacements. The starting equity is an endowment for the test, not earned profit.

At $6,800 not one of 312 cases loses an account, mixed or single-setting, so this state cannot rank the allocations by survival. At $1,500 a full book can still lose every account in a quarter. Adding RR 1.00 at 10% or 20% turns three complete-loss quarters into two and cuts time below five PAs, but those books then spend more time below ten and twenty.

Zero deaths is not the same margin for every sleeve

Per-account headroom of each single-setting book from the same July 2025 start, withdrawals on. All twenty accounts in a book are identical, so each line is every one of them. RR 1.00 falls from $6,422 at the start of February to $741 in the 28 March snapshot, after the 27 March session; a −$171.50 trade on 31 March could have taken it near $570 mid-trade. That is the same March 2026 event behind the earlier $6,800 cliff.

Lowest headroom reached from a $6,800 start, all starts

With no failures to count, the lowest point reached is the way to rank the settings. Withdrawals make every margin thinner, because daily-minimum payouts keep pulling headroom back toward the reserve before a crash arrives. In a capitalised book the mix is only as safe as its weakest sleeve, and here that is RR 1.00, the one the 10% and 20% allocations add. Daily snapshots miss intratrade dips, so each true low is somewhat lower.

03

The reference pair, quarter by quarter

26 synchronized fresh-start quarters, January 2020 to June 2026. Each account starts with $1,500 above a fixed floor, one MNQ; no withdrawals or replacements. A marked quarter is one in which every account of that setting failed.

The pair loses everything only when both do: Q2 2022, Q3 2024 and Q2 2025. RR 0.50 carries it through Q1 2020, Q1 2022 and Q1 2026; RR 2.50 through Q2 2021 and Q4 2023. Two independent books failing this often would share about 1.15 quarters, not 3 — so the two are strongly associated, and the protection is partial. Two other ordinary RR pairs also share exactly three — RR 0.50/2.25 and RR 1.00/2.25 — so it ties rather than leads.

04

Why more RR tuning will not separate candidates

Quarterly failure overlap for every pair in each family, same fixed-headroom quarters.

No pair in any family falls below the product-of-marginals benchmark, so every pair shares vulnerability. The raw ratio depends on how often each setting fails on its own — GG settings fail in 7.46 of 26 quarters on average, RR in 6.31 — which lowers GG's ratio mechanically. Phi adjusts for that, and puts GG pairs as the most associated: for the median GG pair, whenever the less-failing setting fails, the other fails too. That supports the GG-only finding that no GG pair improves on GG 1.25 alone. Cross-strategy pairs are lowest on phi but level with RR pairs on the conditional measure — directionally interesting, not evidence that different signals cause better diversification. These counts are not a significance test.

05

The shortlist ranking depends on which component deploys first

rr_gg_shortlist: two pairs and two triples in the operating model, every assignment order, 2020–2025 starts. Averaging every order gives each component the first seat in 1/n of cases.

The uniform-order average ranks the RR pair first, but a pair deploys RR 0.50 first in half its orders and a triple in a third. With RR 0.50 first, the triples have fewer wipeouts per case and less empty time — weakly, one event each, and the four are separate dates in the 2021 start. The average is not only a proportion effect: the two pairs have identical proportions and still differ, because RR 2.50 first did better than GG 1.25 first. 49 of the 57 wipeouts happen before the book holds five PAs, and none after nine, so this measures establishment risk.

06

The first seat, in all 24 orders of one four-group book

wide4_permutations: RR 0.50, 1.50, 2.50 and 3.50, identical in every run except the order in which seats join groups. Grouped by the value that takes the first seat.

Every order with RR 0.50 first reaches 5/6 or 6/6 clean starts; no other first value exceeds 4/6. Here RR 0.50 first had no startup failures at all — the shortlist shows that does not carry over to every composition. Isolation runs are identical across all 24 orders.

07

Why RR 0.50 first helped: it outlived the early shocks

When each single-RR book lost its last account in the isolation phase — no replacements, bit-identical under permutation. Darker means earlier within that column.

In the 2020 start RR 0.50 is the only value whose book outlives the crash, by fifteen months. Adjacent RR values agree on 5.29 of 6 starts, so the axis holds about three regimes, not thirty-six. That advantage is a fact about this tape's timing; which value survives the next early shock is not something this history can tell you.

08

Reserve ladders spread death dates, not failures

March 2026 under the $5,700 daily-maximum stress policy. Marks are recorded death dates, sized and labelled by accounts lost.

Both mechanisms spread dates — grid20 over four, ladder20 over six — and the ladder's control is clean: rr_1.00, ladder4 and ladder20 all enter March with exactly $76,068 of positive equity. But under the primary policy every ladder reproduces rr_1.00's continuity to the decimal, and it costs deaths.

09

Measurement traps met along the way

Comparisons with no failures

When nothing dies, counts cannot rank anything. Rank by the lowest headroom reached, intratrade where possible: at $6,800 the three settings bottomed $2,000 apart.

Averaging over every assignment order

Valid for a uniformly chosen order, but it does not isolate composition when group size changes how often each component deploys first. Hold the first seat fixed, or report per first seat.

A five-PA threshold for “grown”

Books that clear it had mostly peaked at five to nine of twenty seats. Neither the grouping result nor the shortlist reaches a genuinely established book.

Overlap ratios without marginals

A setting that fails often on its own lowers the ratio without reducing shared failures. Read phi or the conditional rate alongside it.

Worst-day loss fraction

Its denominator is the cohort alive when the window opens, so a collapsed book scores 1.00 for free — partly circular with continuity.

Counting distinct balances

RR 0.75 held seven distinct headroom values with 13 of 20 seats sharing one. Largest same-headroom group is what can die on one signal, and even that is a diagnostic: ladders create balance differences by weakening part of the book.

Claims retired

“RR grouping protects a grown book”

this page, previous version

The “after five PAs” wipeouts were books at five to nine of twenty. Grouping cuts establishment risk; the mature-book study then showed a full book is protected by headroom — at $6,800 even single-setting books lost nothing.

“RR 0.50 / RR 2.50 has the strongest operating continuity”

rr_gg_shortlist transcript

It had the lowest average wipeouts and empty time under a uniformly chosen assignment order — a property of the portfolio with its deployment order, not of composition alone. See the RECONCILIATION.md beside the shortlist findings.

“With RR 0.50 first, all four mixtures tie on one shared event”

first review of the shortlist

Different denominators (1/6 against 1/12) and four separate dates. Conditioned on the first seat, the triples edge ahead.

“Cross-strategy pairs are closest to independence”

first review of the shortlist

Lowest on phi, level with RR pairs on the conditional measure. Directional, not established.

“wide4 6/6” and “spread width is the dose”

rr_followup, and this page's first version

6/6 holds in 3 of 24 assignment orders, median 3/6. edge4 matches wide4 on width, group count, endpoints and mean and still loses a start.

11

What would change a decision

Reserves just below $6,800 for a mature book. Seeded at $6,800 nothing died, but RR 1.00 came within a few hundred dollars. Runs at $6,000 and $6,500 would show where it starts failing and whether the reference pair still holds.

Shortlist comparisons with the first seat held fixed. That is the only way to rank the pairs and triples on composition. The weighted 45/10/45 triple suggested in the shortlist transcript needs the same control, because weights change which group each new seat joins.

New data for the fixed shortlist. Everything here uses history already examined; a forward check is the only independent test of the reference pair.
